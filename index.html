Ko Wai Au · Who Am I

Ko Wai Au

Who am I?

It is the first question a Māori child is taught to answer, and the answer is never a name. It is a river, a mountain, a canoe, a line of ancestors recited back until the names run out.

There is a reason your people never stopped counting.

The Claim

Your culture was never a detour from God.

The Remnant exists to make one argument, carefully and with evidence: that the peoples of the Pacific are a remnant of the House of Israel — not symbolically, not spiritually, but in traceable family lines.

That the obligation to recite your whakapapa, the systems of mana and tapu, the sacred ground of the marae, the seer who saw the missionaries coming from the east — these are not curiosities of anthropology. They are covenant memory, preserved by a people who held onto it more fiercely than almost anyone else on earth.

For two hundred years the Pacific has been treated as a mission field. It was always a homeland.

The Record

This is not a new idea.
It is an old one that was never finished.

For more than a century, leaders of the Church have said plainly what this movement now intends to demonstrate at scale.

The scholarship runs just as deep: Cole and Jensen’s Israel in the Pacific (1961), the first Polynesian genealogical text ever published — and the work of Selwyn Katene and Te Rangihīroa since.

Culture as Witness

Ten things that demand an explanation.

Set the traditions of the Pacific beside the covenant theology of ancient Israel and the alignment is not general. It is structural.

Pacific tradition Israelite parallel

Mana — spiritual power carried through bloodlines, increased by covenant-keeping

Priesthood authority — conferred through lineage, amplified by faithfulness

Tapu — the system of sacred restriction; what carries mana is set apart and untouchable

The law of holiness — what is dedicated to God is holy, not to be treated as common

Whakapapa — the obligation to recite the complete line of ancestors as the foundation of identity

Turning the hearts of the children to the fathers — genealogy as sacred duty

The marae — sacred gathering ground; site of welcome, covenant and judgement

The tabernacle and temple courts — where the community meets God

Tohunga / kahuna — the ordained expert who stands between the people and the divine

The priesthood — ordained intermediaries between God and the people

Matakite — the seer; and the seer stones of the ancient Māori

Seers and prophecy — the same office, the same instrument

Io — the hidden god whose name is not spoken

“I AM THAT I AM” — Exodus 3:14

Tiki — the first man, formed from red earth

Adam, formed from the dust — Genesis 2:7

Whare wānanga — the sacred house of learning, entered only by the prepared

The School of the Prophets — D&C 88

Manaakitanga — hospitality as spiritual obligation; to withhold from a guest is a failure of the soul

Pure religion — James 1:27

These are too specific to be coincidence. They are what survives when a covenant people is scattered and remembers anyway.

The Work

What The Remnant is building.

The film

A cinematic documentary series following real people — athletes, elders, young people — as they trace their own line back through Pacific migration to the House of Israel. Their discovery, filmed as it happens.

The gatherings

Evenings brought to wards, communities and cultural groups: the evidence presented, the traditions honoured, and every person leaving with a name to take to the temple.

The whakapapa work

Genealogical research built specifically for Pacific families, so any person of Pacific descent can trace their line — not only the people on camera.
